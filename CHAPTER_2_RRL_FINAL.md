# 2. RELATED LITERATURE

Recent studies have shown that web-based information systems remain important in improving organizational efficiency, accessibility of records, and monitoring of day-to-day operations. In many service-oriented environments, manual procedures such as handwritten logs, scattered spreadsheets, and disconnected reporting tools often lead to delayed transactions, incomplete records, and weak coordination. Because of these recurring problems, organizations increasingly adopt web-based systems to centralize information, support real-time monitoring, and improve overall service delivery. This development is relevant to electronics repair shops, where job order tracking, repair progress updates, spare parts monitoring, and payment recording are essential to daily operations.

Current literature also suggests that service systems perform better when major functions are integrated in a single platform. Rather than managing customer records, transaction updates, status monitoring, and inventory details separately, organizations benefit from a system that connects these components and allows information to be updated and retrieved more efficiently. In electronics repair shops, this integration is especially important because one repair transaction may involve several stages, such as device intake, diagnosis, parts replacement, repair, testing, payment, and release. When information is fragmented, delays in one stage can affect the whole process.

Another important trend in recent literature is the growing demand for transparency in service operations. Customers increasingly expect timely and reliable access to information regarding the status of their transactions. In service businesses, tracking and update features are no longer viewed merely as optional conveniences, but as important elements that affect trust, satisfaction, and repeat patronage. In the context of electronics repair services, customers often want to know whether their device has already been received, diagnosed, repaired, or prepared for release. A structured web-based system can respond to this need by presenting organized and accessible updates.

Although many studies from 2021 to 2025 discuss web-based information systems, workflow tracking, inventory platforms, and customer-facing service tools, most of them focus on schools, local government units, offices, retail stores, or logistics services. There is still limited recent literature on an integrated system specifically intended for electronics repair shops that combines job order management, repair progress tracking, spare parts monitoring, payment recording, and customer service transparency in one platform. This gap provides a clear basis for the present study.

## 2.1 Web-Based Information and Management Systems

Web-based information and management systems are widely recognized for their ability to improve accessibility, centralization, and efficiency in handling records and transactions. Compared with manual systems, web-based platforms allow users to store, retrieve, update, and monitor information through a browser-based interface, reducing the possibility of lost records, delayed reporting, and duplicated entries. In many organizational settings, the shift from paper-based procedures to digital platforms has improved workflow coordination and administrative performance.

Pocaan (2021) demonstrated the usefulness of a web-based platform in managing internship-related records and workflows. The study found that replacing manual procedures with a web-based system improved monitoring, communication, and handling of reports and documents. Although the context of the study focused on internship management, its findings remain relevant because both internship systems and repair service systems involve stage-based transactions, record organization, and status visibility within a centralized platform.

In a related local study, Simbulan (2022) developed a centralized web-based information management system for a local government setting and emphasized the value of accessible and organized digital records. The study showed that centralization improved service delivery, information availability, and administrative efficiency. These findings are relevant to the present study because electronics repair shops also require a system where customer details, job orders, repair status, and service records can be stored and managed in one place.

These studies show that web-based systems are effective in improving record accessibility and operational efficiency. However, both studies were developed for institutional and government-related settings rather than repair-oriented service businesses. This indicates the need for a more specialized web-based management system tailored to electronics repair shop operations.

## 2.2 Service Management and Tracking Systems

Tracking systems are essential in service environments because they make workflow progress easier to monitor and manage. In operations where transactions move through several stages, tracking systems reduce confusion by showing what has already been completed, what is currently in progress, and what remains pending. In repair services, this function is particularly important because a device typically passes through several operational stages such as intake, diagnosis, repair, testing, payment settlement, and release.

Jaca (2022) explained that an electronic document management and tracking system improved retrieval, record visibility, and status monitoring. The study found that tracking tools reduced inefficiencies associated with manual searching and delayed updates. Although the study focused on document processes, its implications are applicable to repair services because both involve monitoring items as they move through different stages of completion.

Taton (2022) likewise emphasized the importance of stage-based monitoring in an online applicant tracking system. The study showed that tracking systems help users manage multiple ongoing transactions more efficiently and reduce confusion caused by fragmented tools. This finding is highly relevant to electronics repair shops, where several devices may be undergoing different stages of repair at the same time. A structured tracking mechanism helps staff determine priorities, pending actions, and completed services.

Dela Rosa (2022) also showed that a web-based tracking system improved visibility, reporting, and timeliness of updates through centralized information management and notification features. In a similar manner, Berris (2023) found that a web-based membership tracking information system improved accessibility of updated records, streamlined data management, and achieved a high level of user acceptance. Taken together, these studies suggest that tracking systems improve order, continuity, and accountability in service processes. Nevertheless, the reviewed local systems focus on applicants, documents, memberships, and deployment records rather than repair-specific workflows. This reveals a need to apply the same principles more directly to electronics repair service operations.

## 2.3 Inventory and Spare Parts Management Systems

Inventory management is a major concern in electronics repair businesses because repair work often depends on the availability of spare parts, replacement components, and related supplies. Poor inventory control can delay repairs, cause stock shortages, create inaccurate costing, and affect overall service reliability. Because of these concerns, spare parts monitoring should be considered an important component of any repair service management system.

Villanueva (2023) developed an integrated point of sale and inventory management system for a computer store and found that connecting transaction and stock functions improved operational efficiency. The study is useful to the present research because it shows how inventory modules can support business processes beyond basic retail transactions. By linking product movement, stock levels, and transaction records, the system provided a practical model for managing items in a technology-related business setting.

At the foreign level, Maulidi and Prasetyawati (2025) found that a web-based inventory management system improved stock accuracy, reduced mismatches between recorded and actual inventory, and supported better operational decision-making. Their findings are directly relevant to the present study because electronics repair shops also depend on accurate spare parts monitoring to avoid delays, incorrect costing, and service disruption.

These studies support the view that inventory systems improve operational control and record accuracy. However, most of the reviewed works examine inventory as a standalone function rather than as part of an integrated repair workflow. This suggests the need for a system that connects stock monitoring with job orders, repair status, and service costing in electronics repair shops.

## 2.4 Payment, Billing, and Financial Monitoring Systems

Financial monitoring is another important component of service management because it helps organizations keep track of transactions, balances, costs, and reports. In electronics repair shops, this function is essential because the total cost of a repair may depend on both labor and spare parts used. Without a structured system, it can be difficult to determine whether a customer has already paid, still has an outstanding balance, or has only made a partial payment.

Villanueva (2023) is relevant in this area because the integration of point of sale and inventory functions in the study demonstrates how item usage can be connected with financial records. In repair services, a similar relationship exists between spare parts consumption and billing. When parts are used in a repair, the financial component of the transaction should reflect that usage accurately.

Balinado et al. (2021) found that service quality significantly affects customer satisfaction in automotive after-sales service. Although the study does not focus solely on billing systems, it suggests that customer satisfaction is influenced by how organized, reliable, and clear the overall service process is. In the same way, electronics repair customers are more likely to trust a repair shop when repair costs, balances, and payments are properly recorded and clearly explained.

Lin et al. (2023) similarly found that service quality influences customer satisfaction and re-use intention in service transactions. This finding can be connected to financial monitoring because customers are more likely to return to a service provider when charges, service progress, and transaction records are communicated clearly. These studies indicate that financial clarity is not merely an accounting issue but also part of the overall customer experience. Even so, many recent studies discuss service quality and financial monitoring separately. There remains a need for a system that directly connects repair cost computation, spare parts usage, payment records, and service updates in one platform.

## 2.5 Customer Tracking, Notification, and Service Transparency

Customer tracking and service transparency have become increasingly important in modern digital services. Customers now expect timely and reliable information regarding the progress of their transactions, especially when the item involved is valuable, urgent, or personally important. In electronics repair services, customers often ask whether their device has already been diagnosed, repaired, tested, or prepared for pickup. When these updates depend only on manual follow-up, delays and misunderstandings are more likely to occur.

Dela Rosa (2022) showed that a web-based tracking system improved timely updates, reporting, and monitoring through centralized information management and notification features. Although the study focused on security deployment, the underlying principle applies to repair service operations because timely status information is also essential in managing customer expectations.

At the foreign level, Rai et al. (2022) found that the quality of tracking information contributes to customer loyalty. Their study is highly relevant because it shows that customers value not only the presence of tracking features but also the usefulness, reliability, and clarity of the information being provided. For a repair shop, this means that customer tracking should present meaningful and accurate updates regarding the current repair status.

Xu et al. (2024) explained that information transparency improves customer-service matching, reduces inefficiencies, and enhances customer welfare in service operations. Similarly, Wang et al. (2025) argued that operational transparency mitigates customer dissatisfaction in service environments by making the service delivery process more visible. Taken together, these studies strongly support the customer-facing component of the present research. However, most foreign studies in this area focus on logistics or platform-based services rather than localized electronics repair businesses. This leaves a gap for a repair-focused tracking and transparency system suited to the needs of electronics repair shops.

## 2.6 Related Local Studies

Recent local studies provide strong evidence that web-based and tracking-oriented systems are being developed to address inefficiency, poor accessibility, and record disorganization in Philippine settings. Simbulan (2022) and Pocaan (2021) both showed that web-based systems improve record accessibility, communication, and process support by replacing manual procedures with centralized digital platforms. These studies are important because they establish that digital management tools are practical and beneficial in local environments where traditional workflows are still common.

Local tracking-oriented studies also strengthen the rationale of the present research. Jaca (2022), Taton (2022), Dela Rosa (2022), and Berris (2023) all demonstrated that centralized tracking systems improve monitoring, reporting, accessibility of updated records, and user interaction. Although these studies were developed in different contexts, they all point to the same conclusion: digital tracking systems improve workflow visibility and make service processes easier to manage.

In addition, Villanueva (2023) showed that integrating transaction and inventory functions improves operational efficiency and stock monitoring. This finding is particularly important because spare parts management is a practical and recurring concern in electronics repair businesses. A repair management system that does not include inventory awareness may improve records, but it would not fully address one of the main operational causes of service delay and cost inaccuracy.

Despite the value of these local studies, most were developed for schools, offices, applicant management, or store operations rather than electronics repair environments. Very few recent local studies appear to focus specifically on repair shops as service settings that require job order management, repair status tracking, inventory control, payment monitoring, and customer-facing updates in one platform. This strengthens the relevance of the present study in the local context.

## 2.7 Related Foreign Studies

Foreign literature also provides strong support for the major elements of the proposed system. In the area of service quality and customer behavior, Balinado et al. (2021) and Lin et al. (2023) both showed that service quality significantly influences customer satisfaction and intentions to continue using a service. These findings are relevant because customers in service environments value systems that reduce uncertainty, support reliable communication, and improve service consistency.

In the area of service management, Shaheen and Németh (2022) and Feversani et al. (2023) emphasized the importance of structured service processes and practical management approaches for organizations, particularly for smaller and medium-sized operations. Their work supports the idea that service operations perform better when workflows are formalized and supported by digital tools. This is directly relevant to electronics repair services, where repair jobs move through multiple operational stages that require careful coordination and monitoring.

In the area of inventory and transparency, Maulidi and Prasetyawati (2025), Rai et al. (2022), Xu et al. (2024), and Wang et al. (2025) showed that web-based systems improve stock accuracy, tracking information quality, service transparency, and customer outcomes. These findings support the inclusion of spare parts monitoring, repair tracking, and customer-facing status updates in the proposed repair service management system.

Although these foreign studies provide useful concepts and findings, many of them come from logistics, inventory, industrial maintenance, and general service settings. While their principles are transferable, they do not fully address the specific workflow needs of electronics repair shops in a localized setting. This means that foreign literature supports the overall direction of the present study but also points to the need for a more focused and contextualized system.

## 2.8 Synthesis and Research Gap

The reviewed literature shows that studies published from 2021 to 2025 consistently support the usefulness of web-based systems in improving records management, monitoring, service delivery, inventory control, and customer communication. Local studies demonstrate that Philippine organizations benefit from centralized digital platforms that replace manual procedures and improve accessibility, tracking, and workflow visibility. Foreign studies likewise show that web-based inventory systems, service management approaches, and customer transparency features contribute to efficiency, service quality, and customer satisfaction.

The literature further reveals that the major components required in the present study are already supported individually by existing research. Web-based systems improve recordkeeping and access to information. Tracking systems improve visibility of workflow progress. Inventory systems improve stock accuracy and operational control. Service quality and financial clarity influence customer trust and satisfaction. Customer tracking and transparency features reduce uncertainty and strengthen the service experience. These findings show that the proposed study is grounded in current technological practices and practical service needs.

However, the gap becomes clearer when these studies are considered as a whole. Most recent works focus only on one or two areas, such as tracking, service quality, inventory, or transparency, and many are designed for schools, local government offices, logistics platforms, or general service settings. There is still limited recent literature on an integrated web-based system specifically intended for electronics repair shops that combines job order creation, repair progress tracking, customer-facing updates, payment monitoring, and spare parts management in one platform.

For this reason, the present study aims to address that gap through the design and development of a Web-Based Repair Service Management System for Electronics Repair Shops in Surigao City. The study is expected to contribute by providing a more localized and integrated digital approach to managing repair transactions, customer communication, and operational monitoring in the electronics repair service sector.
