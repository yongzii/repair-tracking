# 2. RELATED LITERATURE

Recent developments in information systems have shown that web-based platforms are increasingly used to improve recordkeeping, workflow monitoring, transaction processing, and customer communication across different types of organizations. In small and medium-sized businesses, digital systems are often introduced to replace manual logbooks, scattered spreadsheets, and delayed reporting practices. This shift is important for service-oriented businesses because timely information, organized records, and accurate monitoring directly affect both operational efficiency and customer satisfaction. For electronics repair shops, these concerns are especially relevant because the business depends on accurate job order tracking, repair status updates, spare parts monitoring, and payment recording.

Several recent studies also suggest that modern service operations benefit when management functions are integrated into a single system rather than handled through disconnected tools. Instead of separating customer records, inventory files, payment logs, and status updates, organizations perform better when these functions are centralized and accessible in real time. This idea is closely related to the present study because electronics repair shops often deal with multiple transactions at once, and delays in one part of the workflow can affect the entire repair process. A web-based repair service management system, therefore, becomes a practical response to a real operational need.

In addition, recent literature emphasizes that customers now expect more transparency and faster access to service information. In service businesses, tracking features and timely updates are no longer seen as optional conveniences but as important factors that shape trust, satisfaction, and repeat transactions. For repair shops, this means that customers should not rely only on follow-up text messages, calls, or personal visits just to know the status of their devices. Instead, a structured digital system can help create a more professional and efficient service environment.

Although many recent studies discuss web-based information systems, tracking systems, inventory platforms, and customer-facing service tools, most of them focus on schools, government units, retail stores, warehouses, or logistics settings. There is still limited literature on a single integrated system specifically designed for electronics repair shops that combines job order management, repair progress tracking, spare parts monitoring, payment management, and customer repair tracking in one platform. This gap supports the need for the present study.

## 2.1 Web-Based Information and Management Systems

Web-based information and management systems have become important tools for organizations that need accessible, centralized, and efficient operations. Unlike manual systems, web-based systems allow users to enter, retrieve, update, and monitor information through a browser-based platform, making records easier to manage and less vulnerable to loss or duplication. In many organizations, the move from paper-based records to web-based management has improved speed, accuracy, and convenience in daily operations.

Pocaan (2021) demonstrated the value of a web-based platform in managing internship-related records and workflows. The study showed that replacing manual procedures with a web-based system improved document handling, monitoring, and communication between stakeholders. While the context of the study focused on internship management, its findings remain relevant to the present study because both systems deal with monitoring records, tracking progress, and organizing transactions through a centralized digital platform.

Similarly, Simbulan (2022) developed a centralized web-based information management system for a local government setting and emphasized the importance of accessible and organized digital records. The study highlighted how centralization improves service delivery, information availability, and administrative efficiency. These findings are important to the present study because electronics repair shops also require centralized access to job orders, customer details, device information, and repair updates. A fragmented system can delay operations, while a centralized web-based platform can support faster coordination and better decision-making.

Panugao et al. (2023) also found that a web-based management system with notification features improved recording, monitoring, and communication functions in a local organizational setting. Their study suggests that web-based systems become more effective when they do not only store records but also assist users through timely alerts and status visibility. In the same way, a repair service management system becomes more useful when it not only records repair jobs but also supports updates that are visible to both staff and customers.

Camila et al. (2023) further showed that a web-based management system can improve service administration and user convenience, especially when paired with modern tools such as QR-based identification. Although the study focused on library operations, it reinforces a key principle applicable to the present study: web-based systems help simplify transactions, improve accessibility, and support more organized service delivery. Taken together, these studies suggest that web-based management systems are suitable for environments where records, monitoring, and user access must be handled efficiently. However, most of these studies focus on academic, local government, and institutional contexts rather than electronics repair services, leaving room for a more specialized system tailored to repair shop operations.

## 2.2 Service Management and Tracking Systems

Tracking systems are important in service operations because they make workflow progress visible and easier to manage. In organizations where services pass through several stages, tracking systems reduce confusion by showing where a transaction currently stands, what has already been completed, and what actions still need to be done. This is especially important in repair operations, where devices move from intake to diagnosis, repair, testing, payment settlement, and release.

Jaca (2022) explained that a document management and tracking system improved retrieval, record visibility, and status monitoring. The study showed that tracking tools reduce inefficiencies caused by manual searching and delayed updates. Although the system was designed for document processes, its relevance to the present study is clear because repair shop operations also rely on timely access to records and updated statuses. A device under repair is not very different from a document under processing in the sense that both require clear tracking from one stage to another.

Taton (2022), in an online applicant tracking system, also emphasized the importance of stage-based monitoring in managing multiple ongoing transactions. The study showed that tracking systems help users monitor progress from start to finish while reducing the confusion caused by scattered tools. This insight is highly relevant to repair service operations, where several devices may be undergoing different stages of repair at the same time. A structured tracking mechanism helps staff understand priorities, pending actions, and completed tasks.

Dela Rosa (2022) further supported the usefulness of tracking systems by showing how deployment monitoring improves visibility and timeliness of updates. The study suggests that tracking is more effective when users can quickly determine the current state of a process without relying on repeated manual follow-up. In repair shop settings, this is particularly important because customers often ask for updates regarding diagnosis, parts availability, repair progress, or release schedule.

Berris (2023) added that web-based membership tracking systems can improve monitoring, reporting, and user interaction when records are maintained in one accessible platform. Although membership management differs from repair service management, the broader lesson remains similar: a web-based tracking system provides order, continuity, and accountability in a service process. However, most recent tracking studies focus on records, applicants, members, or deployments rather than repair job workflows. This suggests a gap in applying the same tracking principles specifically to electronics repair service operations.

## 2.3 Inventory and Spare Parts Management Systems

Inventory management is a major concern in electronics repair businesses because repair work often depends on the availability of spare parts, tools, and replacement components. Poor inventory control can delay repairs, cause stock shortages, and create inaccurate costing. Because of this, inventory and spare parts monitoring should be considered an essential component of a repair service management system.

Villanueva (2023) developed a point of sale and inventory management system for a computer store and found that integrating transaction and stock functions helps streamline business operations. The study is especially useful to the present research because it shows how inventory modules can support service-related business activities, not just retail sales. The ability to record product movement, track stock availability, and connect inventory with transactions provides a strong basis for managing spare parts in repair shops.

Baluya and Pineda (2023) also emphasized the value of inventory management systems for small business operations. Their study on a water refilling station demonstrated that a dedicated inventory system improves monitoring and helps prevent confusion in stock handling. Although the business context differs from electronics repair, the principle remains relevant: organized stock records reduce inefficiency and support smoother day-to-day operations.

At the foreign level, Erameh and Odoh (2021) showed that a web-based inventory control system can improve stock monitoring in small and medium-sized enterprises. Their study supports the idea that digital inventory systems are practical even for smaller organizations with limited resources. This is important to the present study because many electronics repair shops in local areas operate on a small or medium business scale and need affordable but efficient systems for spare parts management.

Kusuma et al. (2023) also reported that a web-based inventory information system improved both the efficiency and effectiveness of inventory management. The study suggests that digital inventory systems reduce mismatches between actual stock and recorded stock while improving reporting and accessibility. Likewise, Maulidi and Prasetyawati (2025) found that a web-based inventory management system improved stock accuracy and supported better decision-making. These findings are directly relevant to the present study because parts availability affects repair completion time, costing, and service reliability. Still, most of these studies focus on inventory as a standalone function rather than as part of an integrated repair workflow. This highlights the need for a system that connects inventory to repair status, job orders, and cost tracking.

## 2.4 Payment, Billing, and Financial Monitoring Systems

Financial monitoring is another important part of service management because it helps organizations keep track of transactions, balances, costs, and reports. In repair shop operations, payment records should be monitored alongside labor cost, parts cost, and total repair charges. Without a structured system, shops may struggle to determine whether a customer has already paid, still has a remaining balance, or has only made a partial payment.

Felismino and Ragay (2024) showed that an online point of sale system supports transaction recording and sales monitoring in a more organized way. While their study focused on pharmacy sales, its relevance to the present study lies in the role of digital systems in improving financial visibility and transaction accuracy. A repair shop also needs clear records of charges and payments, especially when a customer pays in installments or when the final amount depends on parts and labor used.

Villanueva (2023) likewise supports this area because the point of sale and inventory integration in the study demonstrates how business systems can link item movement with transaction records. In repair services, a similar relationship exists between spare parts consumption and billing. When parts are used in a repair, the financial component of the transaction should reflect that usage accurately.

From a service perspective, Balinado et al. (2021) found that service quality significantly influences customer satisfaction in automotive after-sales service. Although the study does not focus only on billing, it suggests that customer satisfaction is closely tied to how organized and reliable a service operation is. In repair shops, accurate cost explanations, payment transparency, and clear transaction records are all part of the service experience. Financial clarity, therefore, should not be viewed only as an accounting issue but also as a customer service concern.

Lin et al. (2023) similarly found that service quality influences customer satisfaction and reuse intentions in service-related transactions. This finding can be connected to payment monitoring because customers are more likely to trust and return to a service provider when charges, balances, and service progress are clearly communicated. Despite these useful findings, many recent studies discuss service quality and financial processes separately. There remains a need for an integrated system where repair costs, spare parts usage, payment status, and customer records are connected in one service platform.

## 2.5 Customer Tracking, Notification, and Service Transparency

Customer tracking and transparency have become increasingly important in digital service environments. Customers want timely and reliable information about the progress of a service transaction, especially when the item involved is valuable or urgently needed. In electronics repair services, customers commonly ask for updates about whether the device has already been diagnosed, repaired, tested, or is ready for pickup. When these updates are handled only through verbal follow-up or personal inquiry, service delays and misunderstandings can occur.

Panugao et al. (2023) and Villanueva et al. (2023) both suggest that notification-capable systems improve communication and help users receive timely updates. Although their projects were developed for different sectors, both studies support the idea that digital systems become more useful when they inform users about important changes in status. This principle is directly applicable to electronics repair services, where customers benefit when they are informed that a device is already in progress, waiting for parts, or ready for release.

At the foreign level, Rai et al. (2022) found that the quality of tracking information contributes to customer loyalty. Their study is highly relevant because it emphasizes that customers value not only the presence of tracking features but also the usefulness, reliability, and clarity of the information provided. For a repair shop, this means that customer tracking should not be vague. Instead, the system should provide meaningful updates that accurately reflect the current repair status.

Oetama et al. (2024) also found that online tracking systems and timely updates positively affect customer satisfaction. This strengthens the argument that tracking features are not merely technical additions but are directly related to how customers evaluate a service. Similarly, Xu et al. (2024) discussed the role of information transparency in service operations and showed that transparent systems improve how users experience and assess a service platform. Wang et al. (2025) further argued that operational transparency can reduce customer dissatisfaction in platform-based services. Taken together, these studies strongly support the customer-facing component of the present research. However, these foreign studies often focus on logistics, online platforms, or generalized service settings rather than localized electronics repair businesses. This leaves a specific gap for a repair-focused tracking and transparency system suited to the local conditions of Surigao City.

## 2.6 Related Local Studies

Recent local studies provide strong evidence that web-based and tracking-oriented systems are being developed to address inefficiency, record disorganization, limited accessibility, and delayed updates in various Philippine settings. Pocaan (2021), Simbulan (2022), and Jaca (2022) all show that web-based and tracking systems are effective in improving access to records, monitoring activities, and reducing dependence on manual procedures. These studies are valuable because they establish that digital management tools are practical and beneficial in the Philippine context, even when implemented in institutions with traditional workflows.

Other local studies such as those by Dela Rosa (2022), Panugao et al. (2023), and Villanueva et al. (2023) highlight the added value of tracking visibility and notification features. Their findings suggest that users benefit when systems provide timely updates, easier monitoring, and better communication. This is highly relevant to repair shop operations because customers and staff both depend on accurate and timely status information.

Local inventory-related studies also provide support for the present study. Villanueva (2023), Baluya and Pineda (2023), and Bituin and Tomas (2024) show that inventory modules improve stock monitoring, item control, and operational efficiency. These findings are important because spare parts management is a practical need in electronics repair shops. A repair service management system without a spare parts monitoring function may still improve records, but it would fail to address one of the main operational causes of repair delays and inaccurate costing.

Despite the strength of these local studies, most of them were developed for schools, offices, barangays, stores, or specialized institutional settings. Very few recent local studies appear to focus specifically on electronics repair shops as service environments that require job order management, customer tracking, inventory control, and payment monitoring in one platform. This reinforces the local relevance of the present study.

Foreign literature also provides strong support for the major components of the proposed system. In the area of inventory and stock monitoring, Erameh and Odoh (2021), Kusuma et al. (2023), Chukwumuanya et al. (2024), and Maulidi and Prasetyawati (2025) consistently show that web-based inventory systems improve record accuracy, stock visibility, and reporting efficiency. These studies are significant because they demonstrate that inventory-related problems are common across different industries and that digital systems are effective in resolving them.

In the area of service management, Feversani et al. (2023) and Shaheen and Németh (2022) highlight the importance of structured service management frameworks and technology-enabled maintenance functions. Their work supports the idea that service operations perform better when workflows are formalized and supported by digital tools. This is directly relevant to electronics repair services, where repair tasks pass through multiple operational stages.

Customer-related foreign studies also strengthen the rationale for the present study. Rai et al. (2022), Oetama et al. (2024), Xu et al. (2024), and Wang et al. (2025) all suggest that tracking quality, transparency, and timely information influence satisfaction, trust, and loyalty. In service businesses, customers value systems that reduce uncertainty and keep them informed. This finding supports the customer tracking feature of the proposed repair service management system.

Although these foreign studies are highly useful, many are drawn from logistics, platform services, retail, and other service contexts. They provide transferable concepts, but they do not fully address the specific operational needs of electronics repair shops in a localized setting. Thus, while foreign studies strengthen the theoretical and practical basis of the present study, they also reveal the need for a more focused system tailored to repair shop workflows.

## 2.8 Synthesis and Research Gap

The reviewed literature shows that recent studies from 2021 to 2026 consistently support the usefulness of web-based systems in improving records management, monitoring, service delivery, inventory control, and customer communication. Local studies demonstrate that Philippine organizations benefit from centralized digital platforms that replace manual procedures and improve accessibility, tracking, and notifications. Foreign studies likewise show that web-based inventory systems, service management frameworks, and customer tracking tools contribute to efficiency, transparency, and customer satisfaction.

The literature also reveals that the major components needed in the present study already have strong support individually. Web-based systems improve recordkeeping and access. Tracking systems improve status visibility and workflow monitoring. Inventory systems improve stock accuracy and operational control. Financial monitoring tools improve transaction clarity. Customer tracking and transparency features improve trust and satisfaction. These findings confirm that the proposed project is grounded in current trends and practical system needs.

However, the gap becomes clear when these studies are examined together. Most recent works focus only on one or two areas, such as inventory, document tracking, notifications, or service quality, and many are designed for schools, government offices, retail businesses, warehouses, or logistics platforms. There is still limited recent literature on an integrated web-based system specifically designed for electronics repair shops that combines job order creation, repair progress tracking, customer-facing updates, payment monitoring, and spare parts management in a single platform.

For this reason, the present study seeks to address that gap by designing and developing a Web-Based Repair Service Management System for Electronics Repair Shops in Surigao City. The study is expected to contribute not only by digitizing repair shop operations, but also by presenting a more localized and integrated approach to managing repair transactions, customer communication, and operational monitoring.
